## AI and Leadership Book

A book synthesizing Mitch's LinkedIn article corpus into a unified argument about AI's impact on organizations, leadership, and human cognition. Not a compilation of articles. A synthesized narrative with new material filling gaps between existing pieces. The frameworks in this book are not theoretical. They are drawn from active advisory practice at RoleModel Software, validated through real production work across roughly 40 people and their customers.

**Source material.** 27 published LinkedIn articles (October 2025 through July 2026), full text archived in the social-content repo. Thematic spines already mapped in the content catalog: Human Psychology of AI (how humans relate to and react emotionally to AI), Organizational Readiness (structural and cultural prerequisites for AI adoption), Professional Adaptation (individual career response to disruption), Practitioner Field Reports (first-person accounts from direct experience), and The Mind Series (Codebase Companion framework, aim, synthetic cognition, entity identity). Named MMK concepts with canonical definitions in the Positions and Frameworks Reference: centrifuge, trendslop, institutional cognition, culture machine, shoshin tension, convergent patterns thesis, hybrid trap, encoded mind.

**Two potential book spines** identified in the content catalog: the human psychology of AI, and organizational readiness for AI transformation. Mitch's personal narrative (military, federal IT, science, biotech, policy, software, consulting) is a potential third spine. The Mind Series material (articles 22, 24, 26) covering the aim framework is the most novel and differentiating content.

**Publishing path decision (July 2026).** Self-publish through Unruly Chain, produced to traditional-publisher standards. This decision followed extensive comparative research across traditional, hybrid, self-publishing, and platform (Substack) paths.

Reasoning:
- Traditional publishing is a long shot given modest platform numbers, and the 18-to-36-month timeline has meaningful strategic cost for a book about organizational AI transformation. Traditional is the correct path for Book C (Cuba), not this book.
- Hybrid publishing (Page Two, IdeaPress, Greenleaf) was eliminated on budget. $30,000 to $60,000 is not available for this project.
- Platform publishing (Substack) was rejected because it requires continuous production to retain paid subscribers. Mitch wants to write the book as a sprint and move on, not operate a second content machine.
- Self-publishing is the only path that satisfies financial constraints while preserving timing, control, and strategic value.

The critical condition: professional production is not optional. Developmental editing, copyediting, professional cover design, interior layout, and indexing at a level where the finished product is indistinguishable from a respected business press title. Estimated production cost: $4,500 to $11,000 depending on manuscript length and providers. This is a real investment, not a corner to cut.

**Success metric.** The book's job is not to generate royalties. Its job is to function as the intellectual foundation of the advisory practice: anchoring consulting engagements, workshops, speaking, podcasts, LinkedIn content, and lead generation through David Lusk's distribution infrastructure. If it helps secure even one significant advisory engagement, it has accomplished its primary objective regardless of retail sales.

**Target audience.** AI-curious business leaders, C-suite executives, and founders of mid-market companies ($10M to $150M, founder-led, family-owned, or PE-backed). The same audience reading the LinkedIn articles, extended into a deeper argument.

**Timeline.** Target final draft by end of August 2026. Estimated 3 to 4 weeks for assembly given how much already exists. AI-assisted writing has removed the mechanical bottleneck that historically made Mitch a slow writer.

**Distribution.** Amazon KDP plus IngramSpark for availability. David Lusk's Amplifier campaigns (300+ channels) for visibility. LinkedIn, podcast, and direct client use for engagement. Corporate bulk orders for workshop and advisory contexts.

**Award eligibility note.** Self-published titles are excluded from the National Book Awards but accepted by Axiom Business Book Awards and eligible for Kirkus Prize if they receive a Kirkus star through the Indie program. Foreword Reviews and Publishers Weekly (via BookLife) also review independent titles. This is an acceptable tradeoff because Book C carries the literary prestige objective.

**Asana task.** "Book concept: Hive Mind + Executive Decision + Companion framework" in Content & Publishing.

## Off the Natch

A book project written under the pen name David Ellis Norman. Documents an actual biomedical self-experiment conducted in late 2017 involving AAV-delivered gene therapy (FST344/follistatin overexpression) self-administered at 20 intramuscular injection sites. Total cost under $2,000 including equipment. The experiment predates Mitch's tenure as CTO of BioViva Sciences by approximately 18 months.

The manuscript (partial draft, at least two chapters) interweaves detailed lab journal entries, autobiography, accessible science writing, and a philosophical manifesto about individual sovereignty over one's own biology. Voice is raw, profane, deliberately provocative, anti-institutional. Draws on Mitch's biomedical training (BS Biology Honors, BS Chemistry Magna Cum Laude, MS Bioinformatics, NSF Fellow) and his frustrations with institutional medicine and regulatory gatekeeping.

Core argument: the knowledge and technology to dramatically improve human health already exist, but institutional dysfunction, regulatory inertia, and cultural timidity prevent access. The book recounts what happens when someone with the scientific training to understand the risks acts on that knowledge personally.

Nine years after the experiment, results appear positive.

**Sensitivity.** This is highly sensitive material. The experiment would widely be viewed as irresponsible. Public attribution carries significant reputational risk. The pen name was a deliberate choice. Publication approach is undecided: documentary account under real name, pen name publication, or fictionalized treatment all remain options.

**Pen name provenance and security.** The pen name David Ellis Norman has a documented history. Mitch emailed George Church (Harvard geneticist, one of the most prominent figures in genomics) shortly after the experiment, corresponding under the pen name and its associated Gmail account, detailing the full protocol. This correspondence strengthens the pen name's independent credibility. Liz Parrish (BioViva founder) has seen both the pen name and the first chapter, and knows Mitch's real identity from working together at BioViva. Parrish will not out him. The pen name is not airtight (it depends on Parrish's discretion) but it has stronger provenance than a name invented at publication time. The plan should assume the pen name could be connected to Mitch's identity if the book attracts significant attention, and the reputational risk should be managed accordingly.

**BioViva connection.** Mitch's subsequent CTO role at BioViva Sciences gave him direct professional exposure to other genetically enhanced humans, including founder Liz Parrish (referenced in the manuscript under a lightly veiled alias as "Pretty Liz"). BioViva is a gene therapy company focused on anti-aging and extending healthy human lifespans. Mitch built HIPAA/GDPR-compliant systems and cloud-based clinical data infrastructure there. This professional context provides a credible inside view of the biohacker world that strengthens the manuscript.

**Strategic value.** Potential thought leadership asset in life sciences and biohacking communities. Reinforces the "scientist who acts" differentiation. Publication timing and attribution strategy are open questions. Go-to-market requires careful handling.

## Kelly Interview Project (Cuba Book)

An oral history and memoir project documenting Mitch's father's life story. The father turns 90 in October 2026. He was a high-ranking official in the early Castro-era Cuban government who directed what is believed to be the Gabinete Nacional de Identificaciones (national identification bureau handling fingerprinting and citizen ID). Hired on merit out of trade school. Hired Mitch's mother as a fingerprint technician; they had met in church and were already dating.

The Castro regime punished him with indentured servitude specifically for being Baptist. He was subsequently exiled to the United States. Mitch's mother (died two years ago) worked for the Southern Baptist Home Mission Board after exile, approximately 1971 to 1975. The Home Mission Board is the same organization whose missionaries were imprisoned by Castro in the 1965 Baptist purge (Herbert Caudill, David Fite, 40 ministers, 13 laymen). Both parents and both grandmothers remained active as Sunday school teachers in the U.S. until physically unable.

**The deeper book.** The father's story is the spine, but the book is about two parallel exiles separated by sixty years. The father is punished by Castro for being Baptist. Mitch loses his career for refusing to go along with institutional consensus during COVID and lands at a Christian software company he has no business being at. The father rejected the state. Mitch rejected the church decades ago. Both ended up in exile from the thing they rejected.

Central scenes and images that define the book's tone:
- The foul-mouthed racist father who one cold night in Atlanta put on his coat and walked half a block to tell the unwanted Black neighbors who just moved in that they left their car lights on. Not a redemption arc. The same man, both things, no resolution.
- Mitch creating subtitles of Que Pasa USA for his wife so she could understand that the show accurately represented growing up in Miami in the 1970s, and that yes, that is where his attitudes about male and female roles come from. And yet he took her name.
- Pared, Mitch's childhood pastor, who at a very elderly age granted Mitch the deeply appreciated favor of officiating at his first wedding. Mitch was 45. The church he left still married him, through a man old enough to represent the version of it that earned his respect.

This is not tribute or hagiography. It is inheritance.

**Publishing path decision (July 2026).** Traditional publishing. This book carries the literary prestige objective. Self-publishing would forfeit the award eligibility, review infrastructure, bookstore distribution, library adoption, and institutional credibility that define literary recognition. Extensive comparative research confirmed that the Cuban exile oral history has the highest literary-recognition ceiling of all three book projects, with direct precedent at the National Book Award level (Carlos Eire, "Waiting for Snow in Havana"). The market has not moved on from Cuban diaspora narratives; acquisitions continue in 2025 and 2026.

The book must be positioned beyond "another Cuban exile account." The marketable proposition: a son reconstructs how a senior participant in the early revolutionary government became a Baptist forced laborer and exile, and confronts what faith, complicity, and survival did to their relationship, while reckoning with his own parallel exile sixty years later.

Realistic advance at an independent press: $5,000 to $15,000. The economic return comes from prestige, reviews, speaking, archival importance, and long-term cultural preservation. Timeline from serious commencement to publication: 24 to 36 months.

**Agents to research when proposal is ready** (submission status must be verified at query time): Johanna Castillo (Writers House), Adriana Dominguez (Aevitas Creative Management), Leticia Gomez (Savvy Literary), Nora Gonzalez (The Gernert Company), Dara Hyde (Hill Nadell), Christopher Rhodes (The Stuart Agency), William Clark, Lydia Wills. Mix of Latino-representation specialists and agents focused on political history, religion, and literary family memoir.

**What makes this story distinctive:**
- Two parallel exiles: father punished by Castro for faith, son exiled from career for refusing institutional consensus during COVID
- Government official destroyed for faith, not political opposition
- Indentured servitude as punishment (not imprisonment, not direct exile)
- Mother's post-exile employment at the institution whose people Castro was arresting
- Faith continuity across two generations while the son rejected the church
- Mitch's complex, hostile relationship with his father is central to the narrative (not hagiography)
- Market timing: father turns 90 as Cuba faces its most significant political crisis in decades

**Comparable published works:** Carlos Eire, "Waiting for Snow in Havana" (National Book Award, closest parallel). David Powell, "Ninety Miles and a Lifetime Away" (oral history structural model, Samuel Proctor Award). Herbert Caudill, "On Freedom's Edge" (primary Baptist persecution account).

### File naming

Every artifact for a session shares one stem, matching the source recording:

`YYYY-MM-DD_HH_MM_GMT-4_-_Interview_NN_-_<Subject>_-_Cuba_History`

This is a deliberate exception to the Drive convention of plain English names with spaces. Subtitle
files must byte-match the video filename or players will not auto-load them, so the whole set stays
machine-shaped. Folder names follow the normal convention.

### Workflow, per session

1. **Record** over Google Meet. The recording auto-saves to the organizer's Drive. Google is
   renaming "Meet Recordings" to "Legacy Meet Recordings" and nesting it under a new "Google Meet"
   folder, so check both locations.
2. **Rename** the Drive recording to the session stem and relocate it into `Recordings/`.
3. **Extract audio** to 16 kHz mono Opus at 48 kbps. Verify SHA-256 on both ends of any transfer.
4. **Transfer to Picard.** Two methods available. Preferred: temporary link sharing on the Drive
   file, download with gdown on Picard (`gdown "https://drive.google.com/uc?id=<ID>" -O <file>`),
   then disable link sharing immediately. Fallback: GitHub contents API intermediary (base64 in
   chunks under 10 MB, PUT to `mmkelly-ops/tmp/`, curl raw on Picard, reassemble, verify checksum,
   DELETE from GitHub). The gdown method is faster and avoids git history bloat.
5. **Transcribe** in a throwaway container off `picard-subs:latest`. Never inside the running
   container, which carries a live subtitle backlog.

   ```
   docker run -d --name kelly-transcribe --cpus=7 -e WHISPER_THREADS=7 \
     -v /mnt/user/appdata/kelly-interviews:/kelly \
     -v /mnt/user/appdata/kelly-interviews/models:/models:ro \
     --entrypoint python3 picard-subs:latest \
     /kelly/scripts/transcribe.py /kelly/audio/<STEM>.opus /kelly/transcripts/<STEM>.es
   ```

   Roughly 0.53x real time on 7 cores. A 62 minute interview takes about 33 minutes.
6. **Run a five-minute calibration slice first** on any session where recording conditions changed.
7. **Translate.** Not with Whisper. large-v3-turbo has no translate task, and the task produces
   loose, lossy English even where supported. English is written as a separate translation pass and
   saved as `<STEM>.en.source.json` alongside the speaker map.
8. **Build outputs** with `scripts/build_outputs.py`, which reads the Whisper JSON and the English
   source and emits both markdown transcripts and all four subtitle files.
9. **Upload to Drive.** `create_drive_file` with a `file://` URL only reads from `/root` inside the
   workspace-mcp container, so stage files there first with `picard_shell`, upload, then clean up.
10. **File the Asana record** and update this document.

### Settings that matter

`language=es` pinned rather than auto-detected. Beam size 5. VAD filter on with a 500 ms minimum
silence, because he pauses and Whisper hallucinates into silence. `condition_on_previous_text=False`
to prevent repetition loops across those pauses. Word timestamps on, which is what makes accurate
subtitle cues possible. Cuban and family-specific hotwords plus a Spanish context prompt, which is
what gets the proper nouns right.

### Known tooling constraints

- **Drive copy is broken.** `copy_drive_file` and `Google Drive:copy_file` both return "No approval
  received," including on a 1 KB file. Not a size limit. `update_drive_file` with `add_parents`
  works but **relocates** the file rather than copying it; Google has removed multi-parenting.
- **`create_drive_folder` fails silently at the Drive root.** Always pass an explicit
  `parent_folder_id`.
- **`get_drive_file_download_url` runs in stateless mode** and returns only a 100-character base64
  preview. It cannot deliver a file.
- **`uploads.github.com` is not in the sandbox egress allowlist**, so GitHub release assets are not
  a usable transfer channel even though they would leave no git history.
- **`picard_write_file` truncates above roughly 1 KB.** Never use it for binary or large files.

### Quality gates

Check every session before declaring it done: count of segments below the log-probability threshold,
count above the repetition threshold, and a subtitle validation pass for overlapping cues,
zero-length cues, and cues over two lines. Session 01 came back clean on all of them.

### Standing caveats

Speaker attribution is **inferred from content, not diarized**. There is no diarization in this
pipeline. Uncertain attributions are marked inline and unsplit turn changes are labeled BOTH.

The recognizer writes **`votar`** where Cuban usage is **`botar`**. Every "me votaron" means "they
fired me." Transcripts are left verbatim; the English renders the intended meaning; the per-session
notes file documents it.

Each session gets a `.notes.md` recording recognition errors, garbled passages, attribution
caveats, claims to verify, and threads left open for the next session.

### Shared elements (all concepts)

**Centrifuge (left in all three).** Dark navy background. A large curved arc of glowing orange-red light sweeps from upper left down to lower right, evoking a solar eclipse or the curve of a centrifuge. A bearded man in dark casual clothes stands at bottom left, looking up at the arc. Below the figure, stark white and gray triangular geometric shapes form an angular platform or beam of light. Title "CENTRIFUGE" in large white sans-serif capitals. Subtitle "WHAT AI ACTUALLY DEMANDS OF YOUR ORGANIZATION." Author "MITCH KELLY" at bottom. The subtitle predates Decision 54; the approved book subtitle is "Inside the Emerging AI Organization."

**Shared design DNA.** Solitary bearded figure (representing Mitch), minimalist editorial illustration style, dark sophisticated palette, clean sans-serif typography. Both covers in every concept use the same visual language: a lone practitioner and geometric or natural light as the dominant compositional element. The pair reads as a two-book series.

### Concept D (cover-concepts-v3-20260906.png) ** FAVORITE **

A substantially different art direction from Concepts A through C. Textured, graphic illustration style with a screen-print or risograph quality rather than the cinematic/photorealistic treatment of earlier concepts. Both covers are more conceptually literal and more visually dynamic.

**Centrifuge (left).** Dark navy background with visible grain/texture. A massive spiral or vortex dominates the composition, rendered in concentric cream/white curved lines spiraling inward toward a glowing orange-red core at bottom right. The spiral evokes an actual centrifuge in motion. A silhouetted bearded figure sits on a dark rocky ledge at bottom left, looking toward the vortex. The figure is seated, not standing, which is a distinct posture from all other concepts. Same subtitle and typography. Far more kinetic and dynamic than the eclipse/arc treatment in Concepts A through C.

**Citizen Scientist (right).** Light cream/parchment background with visible paper texture. A dark green/charcoal rocky mass emerges from the upper left with a hexagonal honeycomb pattern on its surface. The rock is fragmenting, breaking apart into smaller stones that transition into a constellation of coral/orange circles connected by thin lines resembling a molecular or network diagram. Solid institutional structure dissolves into accessible data points. A silhouetted bearded figure stands at bottom right holding a tablet or book, looking up at the transformation. Same typography.

**Why this works.** The Centrifuge cover literally depicts centrifugal force rather than just suggesting it. The Citizen Scientist cover directly illustrates the book's thesis: monolithic institutional structure (the dark honeycomb rock) breaking apart into distributed, accessible knowledge (the network of orange nodes). The visual metaphors are precise, not decorative. The contrast between the two covers is also stronger: Centrifuge is dark, dense, pulling inward; Citizen Scientist is light, open, breaking outward.

### Design observations

Concepts A through C share a visual language: lone figure, landscape, geometric or natural light, cinematic mood. They vary the Citizen Scientist palette (warm vs dark) and framing (open landscape vs geometric doorway). Concept D breaks from that language entirely with a more graphic, textured, conceptually direct style where the illustration carries argument rather than atmosphere. Mitch's favorite.
