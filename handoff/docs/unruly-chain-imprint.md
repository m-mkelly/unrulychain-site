# Unruly Chain Publishing Imprint

## What it is

Unruly Chain is the publishing imprint under which Mitch Kelly's books are published. It is not a separate legal entity. M&M Kelly LLC is the underlying business conducting the publishing activity. Unruly Chain is the consumer-facing publishing brand.

Structure:

M&M Kelly LLC
  owns/operates Unruly Chain
  publishes books by Mitch Kelly

First planned titles:

- Centrifuge: Inside the Emerging AI Organization
- Last Old Man

More books are expected. Unruly Chain functions as a durable umbrella rather than a brand designed around either title.

## Why "Unruly Chain"

Conventional independent-press names were rejected for sounding overly academic, respectable, generic, or like vanity publishers. The desired identity was closer to names like Defiant Press or Unreasonable Press: intellectually independent, somewhat insurgent, willing to challenge institutional assumptions, but not juvenile or performatively rebellious.

Mitch already owns/uses the Unruly Chain domain/name.

The name has useful ambiguity:

- A chain of causation that does not behave predictably
- Systems becoming emergent or escaping control
- Actors breaking established institutional chains
- Decentralization
- Independence from conventional authority
- The consequences of capabilities escaping the institutions that historically controlled them

That fits both Centrifuge and Citizen Scientist without defining the imprint as an "AI publisher" or "science publisher."

**Constraint:** Do not reinterpret Unruly Chain as an AI-specific brand, a science-only publisher, or a consulting-company brand. Its value is precisely that it can encompass a broader body of Mitch Kelly's work while conveying a consistent intellectual posture: ideas and actors that do not remain obedient to the structures expected to contain them.

## Book visual identity

The series-level visual direction is mid-century scientific surrealism / scientific editorial design, combining:

- 1950s/60s scientific-journal imagery
- Saul Bass-era conceptual poster simplicity
- Large negative space
- Flat or nearly flat geometric/scientific forms
- Restrained palettes
- Print/halftone/aged-paper texture
- Mysterious scientific or technological phenomena rather than literal AI imagery
- No robots, brains, circuits, glowing AI heads, neural-network cliches, or generic AI-generated sci-fi imagery

A recurring visual device is a stylized representation of Mitch himself somewhere in the conceptual environment. Not a portrait. He appears primarily as a dark silhouette, with his unusually long gray/white beard and large mustache providing the recognizable identifying feature. His pose changes from book to book so he becomes a recurring character rather than a repeated logo.

Current Centrifuge concepts use large mechanical/orbital/concentric geometry. Current Citizen Scientist concepts use cellular/scientific forms breaking apart or escaping an established structure. See `publication-projects.md` for individual concept descriptions and Picard paths.

## Logo

### History

The original Unruly Chain logo depicted a realistically rendered clenched fist gripping and breaking a horizontal chain. Black-and-white, aggressive, detailed, somewhat poster/revolutionary in character. The conceptual content was strong: defiance embodied rather than implied. The hand physically breaking the chain explains "Unruly Chain" instantly.

ChatGPT proposed a UC monogram with a broken chain integrated into the letters. Substantially cleaner, more contemporary, more practical as a publishing mark at small spine size. But it lost some of the force and meaning of the original.

Decision: combine the idea of the original logo with the graphic discipline of the new publishing identity.

### Current concept

A radically simplified fist gripping and snapping a horizontal chain, rendered in a distressed mid-century/woodcut/screen-print style. Beneath it, restrained widely spaced uppercase typography reading UNRULY CHAIN.

"PRESS" deliberately removed from the visible logo. The publishing operation can be described as "Unruly Chain Press" in metadata/legal/publishing contexts, but the consumer-facing brand is UNRULY CHAIN.

Current concept image on Picard at `/mnt/disks/downloads/claude/book-covers/unruly-chain-logo-concept-20260906.png`. This is directionally promising but still a concept, not an approved final logo. The fist/chain detail may still be too complex to reproduce well at actual spine-mark dimensions.

### Requirements

The final mark must work first as a publisher's spine mark, not as a large illustration.

- Recognizable at roughly 10 to 15 mm
- Fist/chain idea needs substantial simplification
- Avoid anatomical detail that disappears when reduced
- Avoid thin chain details that turn to noise at spine size
- Small number of strong positive/negative shapes
- Works in one color before considering accent color
- Works reversed out of dark backgrounds as well as black on light
- Icon usable independently from the UNRULY CHAIN wordmark
- Wordmark usable independently from the icon
- Visually coexists with the mid-century scientific/editorial book-cover system without becoming part of the cover illustration
- Feels like a legitimate independent publishing mark while retaining the underlying sense of intellectual defiance
